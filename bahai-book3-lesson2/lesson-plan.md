# Children's Class Lesson Plan — Book 3 (Grade 1)
## Lesson 2: Justice

**Theme:** Justice means fairness. We treat everyone fairly, respect the rights of every person, and share what we have.

**Class details:**
- Age of children: 5 to 7
- Number of children: up to 8
- Length of class: 45 minutes
- Session: first time on Lesson 2

---

### Timings

| Time | Part |
|------|------|
| 5 min | Welcome and opening prayer |
| 12 min | The quote and whiteboard memorisation |
| 8 min | Talk and story |
| 10 min | The game |
| 8 min | Drawing |
| 2 min | Review and closing prayer |

---

### 1. Welcome and opening prayer (5 min)
Gather the children on the mat, welcome each one by name, and settle them. Then say this short prayer together, with hands held open:

> "O God, guide me, protect me, make of me a shining lamp and a brilliant star. Thou art the Mighty and the Powerful."
> — 'Abdu'l-Bahá

Say it once for them, then have them repeat it after you line by line. We will say the same prayer again at the end.

### 2. The quote and whiteboard memorisation (12 min)

Write the full quote on the whiteboard in large, clear letters:

> "Tread ye the path of justice, for this, verily, is the straight path."
> — Bahá'u'lláh

**What it means (say it simply):** Being fair is like walking on a straight path. When we are fair to everyone, we stay on the right road.

**How we will memorise it (erase a word):**
1. Read the whole quote aloud together two or three times, pointing to each word.
2. Rub out **one word** from the board.
3. Read the whole quote again, and the children say the missing word from memory.
4. Rub out **one more word** and read it all again.
5. Keep going, removing one word each round and reading the whole quote each time, until the board is blank and the children can say the whole quote on their own.
6. Finish with everyone saying it together, proud and loud.

Keep it light and playful. If they get stuck, write the word back for one round, then try again.

### 3. Talk and story (8 min)
Explain justice in words they understand:
- Justice means being **fair**.
- It means everyone gets what they need and no one is left out.
- It means we **share** and we **respect** what belongs to other people.

Ask them:
- What does it feel like when something is not fair?
- Can you tell me about a time you shared, or made something fair?

Then tell a short story: Imagine two friends with one orange. One friend grabs the whole orange for himself. Is that fair? What could they do instead? Guide them to see that sharing it, half each, is the fair and just thing to do. That is walking on the straight path.

### 4. The game — "Fair Shares" (10 min)

**You need:** a small pile of countable items (buttons, counters or dried pasta) and a basket.

**How to play:**
1. Give one child the whole pile and no one else any. Ask the group: "Is this fair?"
2. Let them tell you why not.
3. Give the group a task: share the pile so **everyone gets the same amount**. Let them work it out together.
4. When it is shared out, ask: "Now is it fair? How do you know?"
5. Play a second round with a number that does not divide evenly, and talk about what to do with the leftover (give it away, or take turns).

**Point of the game:** they *do* justice with their own hands. Fairness becomes something they can see and feel, not just hear about.

### 5. Drawing (8 min)
Give out paper and pencils. Ask each child to draw **one fair thing they can do**, for example sharing toys, taking turns, or helping someone who was left out. As they draw, quietly repeat the quote so they hear it again. When they finish, let a few children hold up their drawing and tell the group what fair thing they drew.

### 6. Review and closing prayer (2 min)
- Ask the children to say the quote back to you one last time, all together.
- Ask: "What is one fair thing you will do this week?"
- Close by saying the opening prayer together again:

> "O God, guide me, protect me, make of me a shining lamp and a brilliant star. Thou art the Mighty and the Powerful."
> — 'Abdu'l-Bahá
